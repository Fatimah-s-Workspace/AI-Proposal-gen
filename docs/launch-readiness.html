Start here: the honest verdict

The engineering foundation is genuinely strong — stronger than most products at this stage. CI runs typecheck, lint, 183 unit tests and 67 integration tests against a real database on every push. Authentication has been hardened past what most funded startups ship. Audit logging, roles, and multi-tenancy already exist.

But the core promise is currently not kept on real documents, and that is not a polish problem. Everything else on this list is ordinary launch work. The first two findings are not.

The two findings that change the plan

Blocker 1 · correctness of the core promise

On a real tender pack, the AI reads the first 16 pages and silently ignores the rest

gemini.ts truncates the document at 45,000 characters before sending it. That is roughly 16 pages. Nigerian public tender packs routinely run 60–120 pages.

DocumentCharactersAI actually reads
20-page pack~56,00080%
60-page pack~168,00027%
120-page pack~336,00013%

It gets worse. When Gemini returns anything at all, its results replace the local full-document extraction rather than merging with it. So a large document gets worse coverage when the AI works than when it fails. Nothing is logged, and the user is never told.

For a product whose entire value is "you will not miss a requirement", this is the one bug you cannot ship. A customer who loses a contract because a requirement on page 40 was never extracted has a reputational and possibly legal claim against you — and they will have a screenshot of your checklist saying they were 94% ready.

Blocker 2 · a large share of your market's documents

Scanned PDFs cannot be read at all, and that is most of the public sector

There is no OCR. A scanned tender pack — a photograph of text — yields nothing. The app handles this honestly (it says so rather than showing an empty checklist), which is the right behaviour, but it means a meaningful share of Nigerian public procurement documents are simply unusable.

You cannot market to businesses and then reject their documents. Either add OCR, or make the limitation explicit before payment and route those users somewhere useful.

Where you actually compete

PandaDoc, Proposify and Qwilr are not your competitors, and trying to match them would be a mistake. They are sales proposal tools: templates, e-signature, pricing tables, view-tracking. Their buyer is a sales team sending a quote.

Responsive (formerly RFPIO) is your real comparator — RFP response management, content libraries, SME collaboration. It sells at roughly $20–40k/year, targets US and EU enterprises, and knows nothing about CAC registration, PENCOM compliance, or BPP rules.

Your wedge is not "AI writes proposals" — everyone claims that now, including free chatbots. Your wedge is compliance extraction for African public procurement: the checklist, the evidence matching, the local statutory vocabulary. That is the part nobody else has and a chatbot cannot fake.

Which means the truncation bug is not one item on a list. It is the moat, currently leaking.

The sequence I would actually follow

  1. Fix truncation and merge AI with local extraction. Until this is done nothing else matters, because the product does not do what the marketing will say.
  2. Test it on three real tender packs of 60+ pages and count the requirements found by hand. This is the only measurement that proves the fix.
  3. Decide the OCR question. Build it, or write the limitation into the signup flow.
  4. Invoices, backups, Upstash, email deliverability, alerting. A week of unglamorous work that turns a demo into a business.
  5. Sell to five customers by hand. Not a launch — five. Watch each one use it. Every remaining item on this list will be re-prioritised by what you see.
  6. Then build the content library, because by then you will know whether they reuse answers the way the theory says.

The most expensive mistake available right now is launching broadly before step 2. Compliance software gets exactly one reputation, and the bid that was lost because your checklist missed page 40 is the story that travels.
